# Evidence Ladder

Grade sources and keep investment claims grounded. Adapted from serenity-skill methodology.

## Source Strength Levels

### Strong Evidence
Use for high-confidence conclusions.
- SEC/exchange filings (annual, quarterly, interim reports)
- Earnings transcripts and official investor presentations
- Official customer contracts, order announcements, tender wins, capacity reservations
- Regulatory filings, project approvals, environmental/energy approvals
- Patents, standards documents, technical papers, certification records

### Medium Evidence
Use to support or triangulate.
- Reputable financial media (Reuters, Bloomberg, WSJ, FT)
- Trade publications and industry association data
- Company website, IR pages, product pages
- Sell-side research (when assumptions are visible)
- Supplier/customer cross-checks from public disclosures

### Weak Evidence
Use as leads only. Confirm with stronger sources before high-confidence claims.
- KOL/influencer posts, social media threads
- Forum discussions (Reddit, StockTwits)
- Screenshots with unclear origin
- Unattributed channel checks
- Price action or volume spikes without fundamental explanation

## Evidence Labels
Use in research output:
- **Strong** — filings, official announcements, transcripts, regulatory docs
- **Medium** — credible media, trade publications, specialist analysis
- **Weak** — social discussion, rumor, unexplained price action
- **Needs checking** — important claim awaiting verification

## Per-Candidate Evidence Standard
For each top research candidate, aim to include:
1. One strong/medium source-backed fact about business position
2. One source-backed fact about demand, capacity, customer validation, or financials
3. The main missing proof
4. The clearest condition that would weaken the idea

## Red Flags — Downgrade Confidence When:
1. **Single customer rumor** — thesis relies on one unconfirmed customer relationship
2. **Social-media-driven move** — stock moved mainly on social attention, not fundamentals
3. **Financing risk** — company needs capital raises before the opportunity converts to revenue
4. **Vague customer** — customer unnamed, revenue impact unclear
5. **Inventory/receivables divergence** — growing faster than revenue (channel stuffing risk)
6. **Margin contradiction** — gross margin fails to improve despite claimed scarcity or pricing power
7. **Theme-washing** — management uses buzzword language (AI, quantum, etc.) while segment data is unchanged

## US Financing Risk Flags
For US-listed stocks, also check these dilution/financing risks. They don't reduce conviction directly but must be noted in Risks:
1. **Active shelf registration (S-3)** — company can issue shares at any time without further SEC approval
2. **ATM offering program** — ongoing at-the-market share sales, often used by growth companies burning cash
3. **Large convertible debt** — dilutive above conversion price; watch for forced conversion triggers
4. **SBC >10% of revenue** — silent dilution that doesn't show up in EPS but erodes ownership
5. **Insider selling cluster with no purchases** — management doesn't believe in the upside at current prices

**Where to check:** SEC EDGAR (S-3 filings, ATM prospectus supplements), proxy statement (SBC tables), Form 4 filings (insider transactions).

## When to Apply
- **Always applicable:** Red flags checklist, evidence labels, per-candidate standard, US financing risk flags
- **Supply-chain stocks:** Full evidence ladder + bottleneck scorecard
- **All stocks:** Red flags #1-7 should be checked in every `/research-stock` output
