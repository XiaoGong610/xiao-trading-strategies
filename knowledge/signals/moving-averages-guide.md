# Moving Averages (SMA) Guide

## The Big Three

| SMA | Represents | Who Watches It |
|-----|-----------|----------------|
| **20** | Short-term trend | Day/swing traders |
| **50** | Intermediate trend | Swing traders, institutions rotating capital |
| **200** | Long-term trend | Institutional standard — the one that matters most |

Price above 200 SMA = uptrend. Below = downtrend. Full stop. The market regime table uses "% of stocks above 200 SMA" for breadth — that's how important this level is.

## SMA as Support / Resistance

- **Uptrends:** price bounces off SMAs as support. The 50 SMA is the classic "buy the dip" level.
- **Downtrends:** SMAs act as resistance. Rallies fail at the 50 or 200 SMA.
- **First test** of an SMA after an extended move = likely to hold. **Repeated tests** = weakening, eventual break.

## Golden Cross / Death Cross

- **Golden Cross:** 50 SMA crosses above 200 SMA → bullish confirmation.
- **Death Cross:** 50 SMA crosses below 200 SMA → bearish confirmation.

These are **lagging** — the move is typically 10-15% underway by the time the cross prints. Use them to validate your thesis, not as entry triggers. Don't trade the cross itself.

## SMA Alignment (Stacking)

| Alignment | Structure | Meaning | Strategy Fit |
|-----------|-----------|---------|-------------|
| **Bullish stack** | Price > 20 > 50 > 200 (all rising) | Strong uptrend, all timeframes agree | Buy pullbacks, LEAPS, ride it |
| **Bearish stack** | Price < 20 < 50 < 200 (all falling) | Strong downtrend | Stay away, or short via puts |
| **Mixed/tangled** | SMAs crossing, no clear order | Choppy, no trend | Theta gang territory — sell premium |

This aligns with Weinstein Stage Analysis: Stage 2 = bullish stack, Stage 4 = bearish stack, Stages 1/3 = tangled.

## Which SMA for Entries?

| Trend Strength | Entry Level | Notes |
|---------------|-------------|-------|
| Strong uptrend | 20 SMA pullback | Shallow dips, fast recovery |
| Normal uptrend | 50 SMA pullback | The "standard" buy-the-dip level |
| Weak / early recovery | 200 SMA test | Higher risk — needs confirmation (bounce, volume) |
| Downtrend | Don't buy | Or contrarian at 200 SMA with tight stop only |

## Slope Matters

A **rising** 200 SMA means the trend is healthy — dips to it are buy opportunities. A **flat** 200 SMA means the trend is stalling — more caution needed. A **falling** 200 SMA means you're in a downtrend — bounces at it are likely to fail.

Same logic applies to the 50 SMA. Always check the slope, not just the level.

## Common Mistakes

- **Treating SMAs as precise lines** — they're zones. Allow a 1-2% buffer around each level.
- **Buying the first 200 SMA test in a downtrend** — it often fails on the first touch. Wait for a retest or confirmation.
- **Ignoring the slope** — a flat 200 SMA is not the same as a rising 200 SMA. Context matters.
- **Mixing EMAs and SMAs** — pick one and be consistent. We use SMA across all analysis.
- **Using SMAs in isolation** — combine with RSI (see rsi-guide.md), volume, and price action for higher-conviction entries.
