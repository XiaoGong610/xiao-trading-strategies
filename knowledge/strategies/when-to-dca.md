# When to DCA

## What Is DCA

Dollar Cost Averaging — you invest a fixed dollar amount on a regular schedule regardless of price. Spreads purchases over time to reduce timing risk. Your average cost converges toward the time-weighted average price of the stock during your accumulation window.

**Default schedule: daily.** Not weekly, not biweekly. Daily gives maximum granularity and smooths out intraday noise.

## When DCA Has Edge

- **Conviction is there, but timing isn't** — stock is extended, no clear support nearby, you'd feel sick buying the top.
- **Volatile or expensive market** — CAPE > 35, elevated VIX, macro uncertainty. Spreading buys reduces the damage of a drawdown.
- **Sector in transition** — not clearly Stage 2 yet. You want exposure but don't want to front-run a move that hasn't started.
- **Position building** — you already own some, thesis is intact, want to add more without chasing.
- **Emotional edge** — DCA wins when the emotional cost of a bad entry matters more than the statistical edge of lump-sum. Lump-sum wins ~65% of the time historically, but DCA keeps you in the game when lump-sum would have you panicking.

## When DCA Loses to Other Strategies

| Condition | Better Strategy | Why |
|-----------|----------------|-----|
| IV Rank > 50 | **Sell CSPs** | Get paid to enter at your target price. Premium > DCA's averaging benefit. |
| IV Rank < 25 + high conviction | **LEAPs** | Better capital efficiency — control 100 shares for 15-25% of cost. |
| Stock at clear strong support + high conviction | **Lump-sum** | Support gives you a natural stop. Don't spread what you should grab. |
| No conviction | **More research** | DCA is not a substitute for a thesis. Don't average into uncertainty. |

## Pacing Rules

Adjust daily amount based on technicals and sector context. Base = 1x daily amount.

| RSI | Sector Accelerating Up | Sector Pulling Back (uptrend) | Sector Downtrend |
|-----|----------------------|-------------------------------|------------------|
| **< 30** | 2x — oversold + sector strength | **2-3x — best window** | 1x — oversold but trend is against you |
| **30-50** | 1x — normal accumulation | 1.5x — buying the dip | 0.5x — save cash for the turn |
| **50-70** | 1x — no urgency | 1x — normal pace | 0.5x — no edge, conserve |
| **> 70** | 0.5x — overbought, let it cool | 0.5x — wait for pullback | **Pause** — worst setup |

The best DCA window: stock oversold (RSI < 40) while sector is pulling back within a broader uptrend. Lean in hard.

## Budget & Duration

- **Total budget** — same sizing as Buy & Hold: 3-5% of portfolio for high conviction, 1.5-2.5% for moderate.
- **Default duration:** 20-30 trading days (1-1.5 months) to build a full position.
- **CAPE > 35:** extend to 40-60 days. Slower build, more averaging through potential drawdowns.
- **Daily amount** = total budget ÷ number of trading days.

Example: $100K portfolio, high conviction, normal market → $4K budget ÷ 25 days = $160/day.

## Three DCA Approaches

| Approach | How It Works | Best For |
|----------|-------------|----------|
| **Static Recurring** | Same amount every day. Set and forget. | Simplicity. Stocks with no clear technical levels. |
| **Limit Buys at Support** | Place limit orders at SMA levels (20, 50, 200). Only fill on dips. | Patient buyers. Works well in choppy markets. |
| **Hybrid Zone-Based** | Base daily amount + extra at key support (50 SMA, 200 SMA). | Best risk/reward. Uses technical levels without requiring them. |

**Default: Hybrid Zone-Based.** You get steady accumulation plus opportunistic buying at support. Cross-reference `ma-guide.md` for key levels.

## When to Stop DCA

- **Budget fully deployed** → switch to hold/monitor mode.
- **Thesis breaks** → stop immediately. Evaluate whether to hold or exit existing shares.
- **Stock drops >20% from your average cost** → pause, reassess thesis. Not automatic sell — could be opportunity if thesis is intact, but you need to re-confirm.
- **RSI stays >70 for 2+ weeks** → pause until pullback. Don't chase extended moves.
- **Better opportunity emerges** → redirect remaining budget. Capital has opportunity cost.

## Common Mistakes

1. **DCA into a stock you don't have conviction in** — DCA reduces timing risk, not stock-picking risk. Research first.
2. **No total budget** — "I'll just keep buying" turns into endless averaging down on a broken thesis.
3. **Ignoring thesis changes during DCA** — reassess at the halfway mark. If the story has changed, stop.
4. **Same pace regardless of conditions** — use the pacing rules. RSI > 70 in a sector downtrend is not the same as RSI < 30 in a sector pullback.
5. **DCA when CSPs would be more capital-efficient** — if IV Rank > 50, you're leaving money on the table. Sell puts instead.
6. **Too short a window** — 5 days is not DCA, it's a clustered lump-sum. Give it at least 20 trading days.
